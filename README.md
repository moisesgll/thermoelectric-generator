# Thermoelectric Generator for Residual Heat Recovery

Experimental design, construction and validation of a thermoelectric generator based on the Seebeck effect, aimed at recovering residual thermal energy from compact electronic systems such as CubeSat platforms.

Authors: Moisés Gallardo · Benjamín Farías
Program: Electronic Civil Engineering
University: Universidad Mayor
Year: 2026

---

## Overview

This project presents the design and experimental validation of a thermoelectric generator using a TEC1-12706 Peltier module operated in reverse to convert a temperature difference into electrical energy.

A ceramic power resistor was used to simulate the heat generated by electronic subsystems. A heatsink and DC fan were used to maintain a temperature difference across the thermoelectric module.

The experiment also evaluated whether perimeter thermal insulation could improve the electrical output of the generator.

---

## Objectives

### Main objective

Design, build and experimentally validate a thermoelectric generator based on the Seebeck effect for residual heat recovery in compact embedded systems.

### Specific objectives

* Evaluate the effect of perimeter insulation on open-circuit voltage.
* Analyze the relationship between generated voltage and the temperature difference across the module.
* Estimate the theoretical maximum electrical power that could be extracted.
* Explore the potential integration of thermoelectric energy harvesting into CubeSat platforms.

---

## Prototype Design

The experimental setup consisted of the following thermal path:

Ceramic resistor → Hot heatsink → TEC1-12706 → Cold heatsink → DC fan

The ceramic resistor acted as the heat source, while the fan and heatsink helped dissipate heat from the cold side of the thermoelectric module.

The TEC1-12706 was operated as a thermoelectric generator, with its output connected to a multimeter for open-circuit voltage measurements.

Two configurations were evaluated:

1. Without perimeter insulation
2. With perimeter insulation using electrical tape

The insulation was applied around the perimeter of the assembly to reduce parasitic heat transfer through the surrounding air.

## Methodology

The prototype was tested under two configurations while maintaining the same general electrical and thermal arrangement.

The generated voltage was measured under open-circuit conditions.

The theoretical maximum power was estimated using a Thevenin equivalent model:

P_max = \frac{V_{oc}^{2}}{4R_{int}}

where:

* \(V_{oc}\) is the measured open-circuit voltage.
* \(R_{int}\) is the estimated internal resistance of the thermoelectric module.

An internal resistance of approximately 2.07 Ω was used for the theoretical calculation.

---

## Results

The measured open-circuit voltage increased when perimeter insulation was applied.

| Configuration      | Open-circuit voltage | Theoretical maximum power |
| ------------------ | -------------------: | ------------------------: |
| Without insulation |               0.35 V |                   14.8 mW |
| With insulation    |               0.41 V |                   20.3 mW |

The insulated configuration produced approximately:

* 17% increase in open-circuit voltage
* 37% increase in theoretical maximum power

These results indicate that reducing parasitic thermal losses around the assembly can improve the electrical output of the thermoelectric generator.

> Note: The maximum power values are theoretical estimates based on the measured open-circuit voltage and the assumed internal resistance. They do not represent directly measured power delivered to a load.

## CubeSat Application

The ceramic resistor was used as a simplified representation of heat generated by electronic subsystems such as an Electrical Power System (EPS) or On-Board Computer (OBC).

In a CubeSat environment, thermoelectric generators could potentially be used as a complementary energy-harvesting mechanism, recovering a portion of otherwise dissipated thermal energy.

A practical implementation could involve multiple thermoelectric modules combined with power-conditioning electronics, such as a boost converter and an energy-storage element.

The proposed concept is not intended to replace the primary power system, but rather to explore the recovery of otherwise unused residual energy.

## Future Work

Potential improvements and future development include:

* Direct measurement of the temperature difference across the thermoelectric module.
* Characterization of voltage and power under different electrical loads.
* Optimization of thermal interfaces and insulation.
* Experimental evaluation using multiple thermoelectric modules.
* Integration with a DC-DC boost converter.
* Investigation of energy storage using a battery or supercapacitor.
* Further analysis of the concept for CubeSat thermal and electrical architectures.
